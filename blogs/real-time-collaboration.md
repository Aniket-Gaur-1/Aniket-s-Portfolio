Real-time collaboration is becoming a baseline expectation instead of a premium feature. Users expect drawing, chat, cursor movement, comments, and presence to feel instant.

The core idea is simple: keep the interaction loop short. WebSockets work well for shared application state, while WebRTC can shine for peer-to-peer media and low-latency streams.

The harder part is not sending events. It is deciding what happens when users do things at the same time. A strong collaboration system needs conflict handling, clear ownership of state, graceful reconnection, and UI feedback that helps people trust what they are seeing.

The future belongs to tools that feel local even when many people are editing together from different networks.
